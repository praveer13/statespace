# W0-S03 · Z3 `Fixedpoint` for Stage 7

**Status:** decision record. **Task:** W0-S03 (the INS-datalog decision). **Date:** 2026-10-24 (machine clock). **Run:** {{run:RUN-fixedpoint-equal}}. **Owner review:** 0.5 h.

## 1. Question

Does Z3's `Fixedpoint` engine, run in the Pyodide worker, compute the same relations as Soufflé 2.5 on the S7 fixtures, so that INS-datalog can be Z3 in the worker the tab already loads, with no fourth engine? And, as `curriculum/S07-secrecy.md` SI-6 asks, do derivation heights agree where Soufflé's `explain` output allows? The tracker's acceptance names equal relations; the spine's W0 table (section 13) adds derivation heights. This record answers both.

**Short answer.** Yes, behind an adapter. On 1,885 cases (5,915 relations) the relations `Fixedpoint` computed were Soufflé's, as the SHA-256 of the sorted tuples, in native Z3 and in Pyodide under Node, Chromium, Firefox and WebKit. The heights of derivations rebuilt from `Fixedpoint`'s relations equal the heights of Soufflé's `explain` trees on 53,488 derived tuples, 4,362 of which have rule instances of different heights. A planted fault made the same comparison fail. But `Fixedpoint` is only the evaluation step. Left alone it accepts programs Soufflé rejects, reads a wildcard under negation differently, crashes or answers wrongly when asked for its own derivations, and cannot be stopped by `rlimit` or `timeout` in the middle of a join. The adapter in `scripts/spikes/W0-S03/ss_datalog.py` does those jobs. By the rule DECISIONS.md fixed in advance, INS-datalog is Z3 `Fixedpoint`. The same run shows a naive evaluator about 40 times faster per case in the same runtime; section 5 leaves what to do with that to the owner. {{run:RUN-fixedpoint-equal}}

## 2. Method

1. **Fixtures.** The curriculum has no authored fixture files before W4, so the fixtures are defined here from its text (section 4.1): the toy of LAB-7.1, the reference world W0 of S07 section 1 and its variants, 200 seeded worlds with section 1's sizes, nine S7 policies, Soufflé's transitive-closure example, and 23 programs at the edge of the language. `worldgen.mjs` builds the corpus from the committed fixtures and its seeds.
2. **Adapter.** A prototype in Python, `ss_datalog.py`, that runs natively and under Pyodide. It parses the Soufflé subset S7 uses, applies Soufflé's front-end checks (declarations, arity, types, grounding, stratification), translates a program to `Fixedpoint` (`engine='datalog'`, no parameter changed), reads each relation back with an `Exists` query, and rebuilds a minimal-height derivation for every derived tuple from the relations, checking each step as an instance of its rule. It also holds a naive stratified evaluator, an independent second semantics.
3. **Soufflé.** The upstream 2.5 package for Ubuntu 24.04, installed in a container, ran every case in its interpreter. Where heights were wanted it ran the provenance shell (`-t explain`, `format json`, `setdepth 100`) with one `explain` per derived tuple. Compiled mode (`-c`) ran on five cases.
4. **Fixedpoint.** The same cases through the adapter in five runtimes: native Z3 5.3.1 (the Bench wheel), Pyodide 314.0.7 under Node 22, and Pyodide in a module worker in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, served from one local origin with no COOP or COEP headers.
5. **Comparison.** `compare.mjs` compares each relation as its tuple count and the SHA-256 of its sorted tuples (one line per tuple, columns joined by a tab, a final newline: the form LAB-7.c1 names), and heights tuple by tuple.
6. **Controls and probes.** A planted fault (every program loses its negated premise `!revoked(d, u)` before it reaches the engines) to show the comparison can fail; 23 boundary programs where the adapter and Soufflé may accept or reject differently; and probes of `Fixedpoint` outside the adapter: its own derivation facility, its budgets, and programs Soufflé rejects.

To reproduce, with Docker (the pinned Playwright image), Node 22, `uv` and network access:

```
W0S03_CACHE=<cache dir> scripts/spikes/W0-S03/run-all.sh <work dir>
```

The script fetches and hash-checks the three binaries, builds the corpus, runs everything, compares, and rewrites `runs/RUN-fixedpoint-equal.json` and `runs/RUN-fixedpoint-equal/`. It clears the work directory first and refuses one whose name does not contain `w0-s03`. It takes about 7 minutes. {{run:RUN-fixedpoint-equal}}

### 4.3 Derivation heights

A derived tuple's height is that of Soufflé's proof tree for it: a leaf (a fact, a negated atom or a comparison) has height 0 and a rule node has 1 plus the largest height among its children. Soufflé prints trees of minimal height. The adapter computes minimal heights from `Fixedpoint`'s relations alone, with no help from Soufflé: it lists every rule instance over the final relations and relaxes heights to a fixpoint. It then checks each step of the tree it keeps as an instance of its rule, which held for all 53,488. {{run:RUN-fixedpoint-equal}}

The heights ran from 1 to 11 (30,363 tuples at height 1, 19,953 at height 2, and one at height 11, on the chain of 12). The check is not trivial: 4,362 tuples have rule instances of different heights, and Soufflé's tree had the minimum on every one. On the overlap world, `canread frank q3-plan` is derivable at heights 1 (a share to `frank`), 2 (a share to his team) and 3 (through a nested team); both give 1. Two quirks of Soufflé's `explain` matter to tooling: it prints negated atoms and comparisons as leaves, and it prints a comparison with symbol ordinals (`21 != 18`) in place of the symbols, so the run reads heights from the JSON form and not from the text (`explain-read-ref-w0.txt` is one raw session). {{run:RUN-fixedpoint-equal}}

### 4.8 Soufflé behaviors the labs meet

- **Comments.** Soufflé 2.5 rejects `%` as a syntax error. The code block in `curriculum/S07-secrecy.md` section 1 writes its rule comments with `%`; the files must use `//`. {{run:RUN-fixedpoint-equal}}
- **Facts files.** A blank line is a tuple of one empty symbol in a one-column relation and an error in a two-column one, and columns past the last are ignored. The adapter reads files as Soufflé does, except that it rejects extra columns. In the tab the facts come as tuples, so none of this arises. {{run:RUN-fixedpoint-equal}}
- **Compiled mode.** It needs the C++ compiler the package depends on (g++ 13.3 here) and gave the interpreter's relations on the five cases tried. {{run:RUN-fixedpoint-equal}}

## 5. Decision

1. **INS-datalog is Z3 `Fixedpoint` in the Pyodide worker** [D]. The rule in DECISIONS.md (Brief item 5) and the plan's W0 table selects it: equal relations on every fixture, with the TypeScript evaluator as the fallback otherwise.
2. **It is the evaluation step only** [D]. An adapter owns the rest, and the prototype in `scripts/spikes/W0-S03/ss_datalog.py` is its specification:
   - *checks*: the adapter applies Soufflé's front-end checks before `Fixedpoint` sees a program, and never accepts a program Soufflé rejects (0 of 23 in section 4.4);
   - *translation*: constants are interned into a bit-vector domain of enough bits, a wildcard under negation goes through a projection relation, no `Fixedpoint` parameter is changed, and `datalog.generate_explanations` is never set;
   - *read-back*: each relation is read with an `Exists` query and decoded from the answer formula; any other shape is an error, not a guess;
   - *derivations*: rebuilt by the adapter from the relations (minimal height, each step checked), as ARCHITECTURE section 6.5 says;
   - *budget*: `rlimit` and `timeout` do not bound a join (section 4.5), so INS-datalog's budget is a cap the adapter estimates from the program and the facts before it calls `Fixedpoint` (printed, I10), with `worker.terminate()` as the hard stop. W4-T07 chooses and calibrates the estimate; the 125,000-tuple join took 1.31 s in Chromium.
3. **The fragment** [D] is the one the adapter's header comment lists: symbol and number columns, `.decl`, `.input`, `.output`, rules with positive and negated atoms, wildcards, constants, `=` and `!=`, and facts in the program. The tab says "run this on the Bench" for the eight constructs of the last row.
4. **Heights are gated** [D]. GATE-conformance compares relations as the SHA-256 of sorted tuples and derivation heights with Soufflé's `explain`, which is what `curriculum/S07-secrecy.md` SI-6 proposes and the spine's W0 row already says.
5. **Hand-offs** [D]. W4-T07 builds `apps/instruments/src/engines/datalog/` from `ss_datalog.py` and `scripts/conformance/datalog-souffle.mjs` from `souffle-run.mjs` and `compare.mjs`; this run's corpus is its first fixture set until W4 authors the real ones. W0-S02 can run the same corpus on phones through `browser/` and `engine.mjs`, since its row names `Fixedpoint` in browsers.

**What the data does not settle.** The plan's reasons for `Fixedpoint` were one fewer second semantics to gate and no fourth engine download. The first is weaker than it looked: the adapter needs a rule-instance enumerator for derivations in any case, and the naive evaluator that this run uses for it equals Soufflé on all 5,915 relations in all five runtimes. The second holds either way, because INS-z3 loads Pyodide from S1 on. Measured in the same runtime, the evaluator was about 35 to 45 times faster at the median (0.17, 0.24 and 0.2 ms against 6.34, 10.05 and 9.2) and 3 to 6 times faster on the largest scale world, and its budget would be a step count where `Fixedpoint`'s is an estimate. This record does not make that call: DECISIONS.md fixes the rule, and the review is the owner's. If the owner prefers the evaluator as the tab engine, nothing in items 2 to 5 above changes except the translation and read-back, and `Fixedpoint` stays as a conformance cross-check. {{run:RUN-fixedpoint-equal}}

## 6. Fallback

The plan's fallback is a TypeScript evaluator, differential-tested against Soufflé. It is ready to take over, because everything else is engine-independent: the parser and checks, the derivation rebuilder, the corpus, `souffle-run.mjs`, `compare.mjs` and the five-runtime harness all take an evaluator that returns relations. The evaluator itself is the `eval_reference` function, a loop of about 20 lines of Python over the shared rule matcher; a TypeScript port was not built or timed here.

Switch when any of these happens:

- a Z3 or Pyodide bump changes one relation or height in the conformance run (a bump waits for that gate);
- the labs need what `Fixedpoint` cannot run: aggregates, arithmetic, numeric order, or a wider fragment (section 4.4);
- a sweep that the lessons need is too slow through `Fixedpoint` (section 4.6), or a program the labs allow can exceed a cap that cannot be estimated from its size;
- `Fixedpoint`'s answer shape or its datalog engine changes in a release.

## 8. Open risks

1. **The fixtures are this record's reading of S7.** The curriculum's fixtures are authored in W4; a learner's policy or a seeded mutant that this corpus lacks may differ. W4-T07 re-runs the comparison on the authored fixtures. Equality on a finite corpus is a tested result [T], not a proof.
2. **The fragment against the lessons.** S07 section 11 reads the tutorial's "Aggregation › Counting", and an aggregate is outside the fragment. If a lesson or lab runs one in the tab, the choice in section 5 reopens.
3. **One Z3 version.** Only 5.3.1 was run. Z3 5.3.0 gave wrong `unsat`s where 5.3.1 gives `unknown` [X RUN-z3-11179], and this record's probes show the explanation facility failing in 5.3.1. A bump re-runs this comparison and may change the probes.
4. **Budgets.** A learner's program with a join explosion cannot be stopped by `Fixedpoint`; the estimate and the watchdog are the only guards, and a `worker.terminate()` costs the Pyodide load again (section 4.6).
5. **Cost.** About 9 to 13 ms per case in the browsers is fine for a handful of fresh worlds and slow for a sweep (section 4.6). No phone was timed.
6. **Heights rest on Soufflé's trees being minimal.** Its documentation says so and 53,488 tuples (4,362 with competing derivations) agree with the minimum computed from the relations; no counterexample was found, and none was searched for beyond the corpus.
7. **The reader of facts files** differs from Soufflé's on extra columns (section 4.8); production feeds tuples.
8. **One architecture, one host, desktop engines.** Compiled mode ran on five cases only.
