01 · Four rules the interface enforces
Every claim wears its status Nothing in Plinth says "correct". It says proved, checked to a bound, tested, enforced, assumed or refuted, and it names the bound.
Counterexamples are specimens A failed check is collected, labelled and kept. The vermilion stamp is the most valuable mark in the course.
Call it before you run it Every solver, prover and model-checker run is preceded by a committed prediction. Judgment is measured as calibration.
The tool is not the judge A green kernel check proves the proposition. Whether the proposition is the requirement is always a separate, human question.
02 · Assurance stamps — the core semantic palette
Proved Lean kernel Checked ≤ 2.1M states Tested n = 10,000 Enforced runtime Assumed Refuted trace · 7 steps Unknown timeout 10 s
Proved vs refuted is the pair that must never be confused, so it is blue against orange, solid against solid, and the text is always printed. Checked is proved's hollow sibling: same ink, tinted fill, and it can't render without its bound. Assumed is the only dashed stamp. Dashed lines anywhere in Plinth mean "taken on trust".
