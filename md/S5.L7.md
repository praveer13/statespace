## Read a cycle (15 min)

An agent's audit note reads: "TLC reported no deadlock, so every hold resolves." No deadlock is not progress. A hold that keeps retrying forever has no deadlock and never resolves. This lesson is about the promise that rules such behavior out, and about whose promise it is.

**Question** (before the segment):

The worker retries a capture, the provider may answer "busy" forever, and the scheduler is weakly fair to the retry. Must the hold resolve?

- A. Yes: weak fairness guarantees progress.
- B. No.
- C. Yes, because `Timeout` exists.

**Question** (before the segment):

Which fairness may a claim about your system rest on without saying so?

- A. Weak fairness on every action: it is standard.
- B. Strong fairness on the provider: providers answer eventually.
- C. None: each fairness conjunct is an assumption to state.

A spec allows every behavior its actions allow, including behaviors that stutter forever or never take a particular action. **Fairness** is a conjunct you add to `Spec` to rule such behaviors out. An action is *enabled* in a state when it can be taken from there. Two forms cover nearly everything:

- `WF_v(A)`, weak fairness: if `A` stays enabled continuously from some point on, `A` is eventually taken.
- `SF_v(A)`, strong fairness: if `A` is enabled infinitely often, `A` is eventually taken.

Written out, `WF_v(A) == <>[](ENABLED <<A>>_v) => []<><<A>>_v` and `SF_v(A) == []<>(ENABLED <<A>>_v) => []<><<A>>_v`. Strong fairness forces `A` in more behaviors, so it is the larger promise.

On a lasso whose cycle is C, the two have a simple reading rule. `WF` on A holds when A is taken somewhere on C, or A is disabled at some state of C. `SF` on A holds when A is taken on C, or A is disabled at every state of C.

Two common slips. Weak fairness does not mean "taken eventually if ever enabled": an action that is disabled now and then escapes it. And an enabled action does not happen on its own: without a fairness conjunct, a behavior may never take it.

Every fairness conjunct is an assumption, and the first question to ask of one is whose action it sits on. Put fairness on your own system's actions, such as the retry job your scheduler runs. "The provider answers eventually" is a wish about someone else's system, and a claim that rests on it has to say so.

**A worked example, start to finish.** Take the retrying hold from the last lesson and let expiry apply while a request is outstanding, with weak fairness on expiry alone:

```tla
---- MODULE HoldLive ----
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES st, tries
vars == <<st, tries>>
Init == st = "held" /\ tries = 0
Try     == st = "held"    /\ st' = "pending"  /\ tries' = (tries + 1) % 2
Busy    == st = "pending" /\ st' = "held"     /\ UNCHANGED tries
Capture == st = "pending" /\ st' = "captured" /\ UNCHANGED tries
Timeout == st \in {"held", "pending"} /\ st' = "released" /\ UNCHANGED tries
Next == Try \/ Busy \/ Capture \/ Timeout
Fair == WF_vars(Timeout)
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_vars /\ Fair
Resolved == st \in {"captured", "released"}
EventuallyResolves == <>Resolved
====
```

1. **The action and when it is enabled.** `Timeout` is enabled whenever `st` is `held` or `pending`, which is whenever the hold is unresolved. In the last lesson it applied only in `held`.
2. **The safety side still holds.** Every resolution requires an unresolved state, so a resolved hold stays resolved.
3. **The fairness conjunct.** `Fair == WF_vars(Timeout)`, on a system action only. Nothing here assumes the provider answers. Fairness is a promise. Whose? Yours: your scheduler runs your expiry job.
4. **The property.** `EventuallyResolves == <>Resolved`.
5. **The configuration and the stamp.** `SPECIFICATION Spec`, `PROPERTY EventuallyResolves`, no SYMMETRY and no CONSTRAINT, and the deadlock check off, because a resolved hold has nothing left to do (run RUN-tlc-eventually). The stamp is [M] · constants; fairness as [A].

## Weak or strong (15 min)

Now change the promise and watch the lasso. Weak fairness forces an action that stays enabled. Strong fairness forces an action that keeps coming back enabled, even if something disables it in between. Decide with the reading rule first, then run.

Look at what that cost. `Capture` is the provider accepting, so `SF_vars(Capture)` promises that an acceptance, offered again and again, is eventually taken. It removes the cycle by assuming something about the provider's side. Keep fairness on your own actions.

A design that keeps the promise on your side can still fail. Try `WF_vars(Try) /\ SF_vars(Timeout)`, with `Timeout` enabled only at `held`. A provider that stays silent leaves the hold `pending` forever, and `Timeout` is disabled there, so strong fairness has nothing to force (run RUN-tlc-hold-fairness).

- D. None: strong fairness on `Timeout` makes the hold resolve.
