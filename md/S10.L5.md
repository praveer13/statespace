# S10.L5 · Systematic testing

> Draft: first-learner pass pending.

**Format.** Capstone step, about 9 minutes.

**Objective.** Run seeded differential and fault-injection suites on your Core, and let every failure become a specimen.

**Ideas taught.**

- Implementation traces checked as behaviors of the spec
- Seeded fault schedules replay the same transitions
- What differential tests establish: outcome classes, n, generator statistics

**Segments.**

1. Recall before you wire
2. Re-run your gate

## Recall before you wire (3 min)

Your simulator replays a schedule of faults from a seed. Trace validation then asks TLC whether the trace your simulator produced is a behavior of your spec. Both are tests of your implementation, and a report that merges them with the spec's own model check loses what each one covers.

**Question** (before the segment):

Your simulator's traces pass TLC trace validation on n seeds. What does the report print?

- A. PROVED [P]: no seed failed, and the traces are right.
- B. TESTED [T] · n seeds, kept apart from the spec's MODEL-CHECKED [M].
- C. MODEL-CHECKED [M] · n, as the seeds bound the exploration of the spec.

## Re-run your gate (6 min)

The gate from Stage 8 asks for two things (LAB-8.c7). Every seed is recorded, and every failure is mounted as a specimen. A seed makes a run replayable: the same seed gives the same schedule of faults and the same transitions. "Deterministic" does not mean "uses no randomness". It means the randomness comes from a seed you record, so a failure can be run again.

The wired suites print n, the seeds and the generator statistics. In the Core, the differential suite checks outcome classes against the interpreter, and the simulation suite checks traces against your spec. A failure in either is a witness. It becomes a specimen, labeled from the run that found it.

The cell is one trace from a recorded run, read by TLC's trace validation, one line at a time. Each line is an event of the outbox machine. TLC asks whether the event is a step your spec allows from the state so far, and stops at the first line that is not.

**Cell 1** (INS-bench-tlc)

Keep the two tests apart in your report. The spec's model check says what the design allows, over its constants. Trace validation says that your implementation's traces were behaviors of that design, on n seeds, and finite traces cannot test that something eventually happens. A claim about progress keeps its correspondence as an assumption.
