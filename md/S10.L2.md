## Recall before you wire (3 min)

Your Rust interpreter decides what every command does inside the Core, and an answer like that needs something above it to be measured against. Stage 3 gave it one: goldens, theorems that the Lean kernel checked about the reference semantics. Before you wire the interpreter in, say what its agreement with them is worth.

**Question** (before the segment):

Your Rust interpreter agrees with the kernel-checked goldens on n fresh seeds. Which stamp does that earn?

- A. TESTED [T] · n goldens, resting on the pinned Lean reference.
- B. PROVED [P] · the Lean kernel, because the goldens are theorems.
- C. MODEL-CHECKED [M] · n, because the seeds bound the check.

## Re-run your gate (6 min)

The oracle is the Lean semantics. Its goldens have the form `run c s₀ = r`, and the kernel checked every one of them (S3.L9). Your interpreter is the thing under test. It earns its stamp by agreeing with the goldens, and it cannot earn more than that agreement does.

The gate from Stage 3 is exact about what agreeing means (LAB-3.c10). It draws a seed when it runs, samples the goldens under the policy you declared, spreads the sample across operations and outcome classes, includes boundary values, and requires that every outcome equals the golden's. It prints the number of samples and the count for each stratum.

Your four decision records from Stage 3 matter here. Zero, self-transfer, failed guard and overflow each have an answer you chose, and the goldens select their expected outcomes by the policy you declared. An interpreter that follows a different policy than the one declared disagrees with the goldens on exactly those cases.

**Cell 1** (INS-lane-a)

Your interpreter runs each call from the start state, under the policy you declared. The golden for each call holds the outcome and the resulting state for that policy. You compare both.

```text
start      mint -100 · a 100 · b 0
zero       Transfer(a, b, 0)
self       Transfer(a, a, 30)
refused    Transfer(a, b, 130)
overflow   Deposit(b, 1), with b at the bound you chose
```

Agreement over n goldens is a test, so the claim you carry is TESTED [T] · n goldens, resting on the pinned Lean reference, and n is printed. Sampled seeds never turn a test into PROVED [P], however many pass.

The interpreter you meet in the tab is a port of the reference. The course tests that port against the same goldens, and every "re-checked here" label in your report rests on that agreement (SEAM-13). Your own interpreter has its own claim, and it is this one.
