# S7.L1 · Access models in the roadmap's nouns

> Draft: first-learner pass pending.

**Format.** Short lesson with reading, about 18 minutes.

**Objective.** Decide which reads the roadmap's sharing rule allows on a small world, and tell role-based, attribute-based and relationship-based rules apart.

**Ideas taught.**

- Users, teams, roles, documents and sharing

**Segments.**

1. The nouns
2. Reading a rule on a world
3. Exit

## The nouns (3 min)

Your system now has tenants, teams, users, roles, documents and sharing. The roadmap states the read rule in one sentence: a user may see a document when they are a team member or it is explicitly shared. Whatever no rule allows is denied.

**Question** (before the segment):

frank is in Acme, on Growth. q3-plan belongs to Payments, also in Acme, and nobody has shared it with him. Under the roadmap's rule, in a system that denies by default, can frank see q3-plan?

- A. Yes: nothing in the rule forbids it.
- B. No: neither way in applies to him.
- C. Yes: he and Payments share a tenant.

## Reading a rule on a world (12 min)

A policy here is facts and rules. A fact records one relationship: a membership, an owner, a share. A rule says when a new fact follows from others. The engine derives every fact the rules allow and nothing else, which is default deny. A premise written `!revoked(d, u)` must be absent.

Rules differ in what they consult. A role-based rule asks what you are: admins may delete. An attribute-based rule compares properties: clearance at or above the label. A relationship-based rule follows a path through the data, from user to team to document. The roadmap's rule is relationship-based, and it has three ways in.[^s2b1-1]

[^s2b1-1]: In Zanzibar the recursion in such paths lives in the engine, in Cedar in the data (each entity carries its ancestors), and in Datalog in the rules. S7.L3 shows why that matters to a checker.

The toy below relates users straight to documents. Make your call before you run it.

**Cell 1** (INS-datalog)

Query asks: is `canread(1, 4)` derivable? `sat` means yes, so user 1 may read document 4.

```datalog
.decl member(u: number, d: number)
.decl shared(u: number, d: number)
.decl revoked(u: number, d: number)
.decl canread(u: number, d: number)
.output canread

member(1, 3).
shared(1, 4).
revoked(1, 4).

canread(u, d) :- member(u, d).
canread(u, d) :- shared(u, d), !revoked(u, d).
```

**Cell 2** (INS-datalog)

Query asks: which (user, document) pairs are derivable? The cell prints `canread` as a matrix of users and documents.

```datalog
.decl tenant_of_team(t: symbol, a: symbol)
.decl tenant_of_user(u: symbol, a: symbol)
.decl member(u: symbol, t: symbol, r: symbol)
.decl owns(t: symbol, d: symbol)
.decl share_user(d: symbol, u: symbol)
.decl share_team(d: symbol, t: symbol)
.decl revoked(d: symbol, u: symbol)
.decl canread(u: symbol, d: symbol)
.output canread

// the world: two tenants, three teams, four users, four documents
tenant_of_team("payments", "acme").  tenant_of_team("growth", "acme").  tenant_of_team("legal", "birch").
tenant_of_user("dana", "acme").  tenant_of_user("frank", "acme").
tenant_of_user("gia", "birch").  tenant_of_user("hal", "birch").
member("dana", "payments", "editor").  member("frank", "growth", "viewer").
member("gia", "legal", "admin").       member("hal", "legal", "viewer").
owns("payments", "q3-plan").  owns("growth", "roadmap").
owns("legal", "contract").    owns("legal", "layoffs-memo").
share_user("contract", "dana").
share_team("roadmap", "payments").

// rule 1: a member of the owning team
canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, _), owns(t, d).
// rule 2: shared with the user, and not revoked
canread(u, d) :- share_user(d, u), !revoked(d, u).
// rule 3: shared with one of the user's teams
canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, _), share_team(d, t).
```
