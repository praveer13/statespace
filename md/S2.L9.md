## A green build, and then what (2 min)

A green build feels like an answer. In Lean it is not one, and this lesson is about the gap: what each check actually looked at, what a proof quietly assumes, and what happened in 2026 when a checker itself was wrong.

## Four kinds of axiom (13 min)

A Lean proof is a term, and the kernel checks that the term has the type of the statement. The term may lean on assumptions that Lean does not prove: axioms. `#print axioms` lists every axiom a theorem depends on, following the dependencies all the way down. It re-checks nothing. It tells you what the proof assumes, and the assumptions fall into four kinds.

| Kind | The list shows | The stamp it earns |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | some of `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` | PROVED [P] · Lean 4.35.0 kernel · axioms listed |
| Unfinished | `sorryAx` | no [P]: "PROVED modulo k assumed lemmas", each lemma ASSUMED [A] |
| Native | a name containing `_native` | PROVED [P] · kernel + compiler |
| Custom | an axiom someone declared | no [P]: the axiom is carried as ASSUMED [A], like `sorryAx` |

Here are five theorems, one for each kind and one for a tactic that adds nothing. Read the code, then make your call before you look at what `#print axioms` says about the native one.

## Build, axioms, replay (14 min)

Three checks sound alike and are not. A **build** elaborates your files: it runs the tactics, and it reports errors. An **axiom list** says what the finished proof assumes. A **kernel replay** takes every declaration in the environment and checks it again, however it got there. The difference matters, because the elaborator is not the kernel.

Three specimens from recorded runs show the gaps:

- A file whose theorem ends in `sorry` builds with a warning, and its axiom list names `sorryAx` (run RUN-lean-axioms). A green build is no verdict on a proof.
- On a project with three non-standard axioms, `lake check` rejects each of them, and its log still prints `Lean default kernel accepts the solution` (run RUN-lean-axioms). Those are two verdicts: the kernel accepted the proofs, and the axiom policy refused three assumptions. Read every line.
- A declaration added while kernel checking is switched off by the option `debug.skipKernelTC`, and the theorem `1 = 2` built on it, both build clean and list no axioms (run RUN-lean-skipkernel). The source stays out of this page on purpose. The option line is the point.

## The day the kernel was wrong (14 min)

One kernel is one place to trust. Comparator can run several: `--paranoid` checks your solution with every checker the toolchain bundles, and on a solution like yours it takes seconds (run RUN-lean-comparator). Its longer sibling, `lake check --paranoid`, replays the whole environment and takes minutes on a fast machine, so this lesson names it and does not wait for it (run RUN-bwrap-lanes).

In July 2026 an AI-assisted Lean "disproof" of the Collatz conjecture was published from a pipeline that replayed proofs, checked axioms and ran a pinned second checker. Run the check on your own project, and make your call about that pipeline before you read what happened.

> **The day the kernel was wrong (Dated incident)**
>
> On 2026-07-25 a Lean "disproof" of the Collatz conjecture was published from a pipeline that replayed proofs, checked axioms and ran a pinned second checker, nanoda. On 2026-07-28 issue #14576 reduced it to a proof of `False` with no axioms, and the kernel was fixed the same day. The postmortem of 2026-08-01 found two bugs, chained: the official kernel "had a missing check in the nested inductive type support", and nanoda "did not verify the type name in a projection node". The repository "also passed a week-old version of nanoda". A third checker, lean4lean, shared the kernel's bug because its inductive types port the reference implementation. The advisory of 2026-08-02 says exploiting it "requires execution of a metaprogram within the Lean process", which is why `lake check` runs your build in a sandbox and replays an export of the result. Sources: [the postmortem](https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/2026-8-1-postmortem-for-kernel-soundness-bug-14576/) and [the advisory](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/08/02/1).

## Exit (5 min)

**Assumed, and tested at every Lean Defence:** the Lean 4.35.0 kernel accepts only well-typed terms. That is SEAM-15, and it sits under every claim you mint in this stage. It is held, not cracked. If an advisory ever reopens it, the news reaches you as a note on your claims, never as a silent change to them.

**Say it back.** Retell the story in four sentences, without exploit detail: what the pipeline checked, what was wrong, why a second checker did not save it, and what the fix and the advisory changed. Nothing here is graded.
