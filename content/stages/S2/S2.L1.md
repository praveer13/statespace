## Play, then read a proof {minutes=15 kcs=KC-2.induction-principle}

:::item role=prequestion family=GEN-proof-structure template=t3 kcs=KC-2.induction-principle
@choice key=A
You prove a claim P(n) for every natural number n by induction. In the step from d to d + 1, what may you assume?

- A. P(d)
  - why: The step proves P(d + 1) from P(d). With the base case P(0), every n is covered.
- B. P(d + 1)
  - why: That is the goal of the step. Assuming it is circular.
  - mis: dp:L6
- C. P(n) for every n
  - why: That is the theorem itself, not something you are given.
  - mis: dp:L6
- D. Nothing, because P(0) already covers it
  - why: The base case covers 0 only. The step is what carries the claim to every other number.
  - mis: dp:L6
:::

Induction proves a claim about every natural number without checking them one by one. The **base case** shows the claim for 0. The **step** shows that if the claim holds for some `d`, it holds for `d + 1`. Together they cover every `n`, like a row of dominoes: the first one falls, and each one knocks over the next.

The step is not circular. Its assumption is the claim for `d` and its goal is the claim for `d + 1`, so you are assuming one smaller case, not the thing you want to prove. The base case is not optional either: the step only says "if", and without a first case nothing ever falls.

[The Natural Number Game](https://adam.math.hhu.de/#/g/leanprover-community/NNG4) teaches this by making you rebuild arithmetic from `0` and `succ`, the successor. It is a separate site that runs in your browser, so the link leaves this course: the course does not see your progress and does not grade it. Play its first four worlds, Tutorial, Addition, Multiplication and Implication, as far as is useful. Plan three to five hours; that time belongs to the stage's build budget, not to this lesson. The game runs its own Lean version, so a few tactics behave differently on the Bench. If you would rather not leave the course, drawer D-08 holds a pinned fallback. Nothing in the course depends on how far you get.

Back here, read one induction in the shape you just practised. `credit2 k` is the total after `k` deposits of 2, and the theorem says it equals `k + k`. In the replay the `zero` case is the base case and the `succ` case is the step, with `ih` the claim for `k`. Make your call before the step is shown.

:::instrument ins=INS-goal-replay cell=1 recorded=RUN-lean-s2-cells
```lean
def credit2 : Nat → Nat
  | 0 => 0
  | k + 1 => credit2 k + 2

theorem credit2_eq (k : Nat) : credit2 k = k + k := by
  induction k with
  | zero => rfl
  | succ k ih =>
    unfold credit2
    rw [ih]
    omega
```

@direction
Replay shows: the proof of `credit2_eq`, one tactic at a time. The `zero` case is the base case; in the `succ` case, `ih` is the claim for `k`.

@call key=A surprise=no run=RUN-lean-s2-cells
Which fact closes the `k + 1` case after `credit2` is unfolded?

- A. `ih : credit2 k = k + k`
  - why: Unfolding leaves `credit2 k + 2`. Rewriting with the claim for `k` leaves plain arithmetic.
- B. `credit2 (k + 1) = (k + 1) + (k + 1)`
  - why: That is the goal of this case, so it cannot be the tool that proves it.
  - mis: dp:L6
- C. `credit2 0 = 0`
  - why: The base case says nothing about `k`.
  - mis: dp:L6
- D. None: `rfl` closes it
  - why: With a variable `k`, computation alone does not reach the claim.
  - mis: dp:L2

@after
The step unfolds `credit2 (k + 1)` to `credit2 k + 2`, rewrites `credit2 k` with `ih`, and finishes with arithmetic {{run:RUN-lean-s2-cells}}. Take `ih` away and the case cannot be proved: it is the whole content of the step.
:::

:::item role=segment family=GEN-proof-structure template=t3 kcs=KC-2.induction-principle
@choice key=B
`fees k` adds up `k` fees of 3, so `fees 0 = 0` and `fees (k + 1) = fees k + 3`. To prove `fees k = 3 * k` by induction on `k`, which fact does the `k + 1` case need?

- A. `fees (k + 1) = 3 * (k + 1)`
  - why: That is the goal of the case. Assuming it is circular.
  - mis: dp:L6
- B. `ih : fees k = 3 * k`
  - why: Unfolding leaves `fees k + 3`. The claim for `k` turns it into `3 * k + 3`, which is `3 * (k + 1)`.
- C. `fees 0 = 0`
  - why: The base case covers 0 only and says nothing about `k`.
  - mis: dp:L6
- D. `Nat.mul_comm`
  - why: A true lemma, but nothing in this case needs it. The step needs the hypothesis.
  - mis: dp:X2
:::

## Exit {minutes=5 kcs=KC-2.induction-principle}

:::item role=exit family=GEN-proof-structure template=t3 kcs=KC-2.induction-principle
@choice key=C
`grow` doubles at each step: `grow 0 = 1` and `grow (k + 1) = grow k + grow k`. To prove `0 < grow k` by induction on `k`, which fact does the `k + 1` case need?

- A. `0 < grow (k + 1)`
  - why: That is the goal of the case. Assuming it is circular.
  - mis: dp:L6
- B. `grow 0 = 1`
  - why: It starts the induction in the base case. The step needs the claim for `k`.
  - mis: dp:L6
- C. `ih : 0 < grow k`
  - why: Unfolding leaves `grow k + grow k`, a sum of two positive numbers once `ih` is used.
- D. `Nat.add_comm`
  - why: A true lemma that this case never needs.
  - mis: dp:X2
:::
