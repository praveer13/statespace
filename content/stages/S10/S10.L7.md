## Before you export {minutes=5 kcs=KC-10.claim-graph-report,KC-10.defeater-log,KC-10.publish-limitations}

Your repo holds every claim you have minted since Project 00. Once published, your Core is a research artifact in its own right, whether or not it ever becomes part of a larger system. A stranger will read one report. What may its first page say?

Two questions first. The segments after this one answer both.

:::item role=prequestion family=GEN-call-it template=t4 kcs=KC-10.claim-graph-report,KC-10.publish-limitations
@choice key=C
Your Core holds claims stamped PROVED [P], MODEL-CHECKED [M] and TESTED [T]. Which first-page summary may the report print?

- A. "The Core is verified."
  - why: A bare "verified" erases the bounds, the assumptions and the checkers. The report's vocabulary leaves it out.
  - mis: dp:X8
- B. "80% of its claims are proved."
  - why: A percentage across stamps counts MODEL-CHECKED [M] and TESTED [T] claims as unfinished PROVED [P] ones.
  - mis: dp:X6
- C. The claims by stamp, each with its qualifier, plus what is assumed and what is not covered, each linked to its lines.
  - why: The summary is the walk of the claim graph, shortened. It is never a score.
- D. "All tests and proofs pass."
  - why: Pass or fail hides what each check established, and its bound.
  - mis: dp:X1
:::

## Where each claim goes {minutes=13 kcs=KC-10.claim-graph-report}

You do not write the report. You export it: `statespace export` walks your claim graph and places every claim, and `statespace check` then confirms that the walk followed the rules and that every stamp re-derives from its receipt. You write the propositions, the assumptions, the doubts and the limitations. The tool decides where each one goes.

The export writes these sections, in this order: a title block with the edition pins, the commit and the bundle hash, and no claim about who you are; what this report establishes and what it does not (CL-10.2); the Core and its trusted base; the operational semantics and your four decision records; the theorem statements with their hashes; PROVED; MODEL-CHECKED; TESTED; ENFORCED; CONFIGURED; ASSUMED; NOT COVERED; UNKNOWN; the specimens; the demonstration (CL-10.1); the erosion log; the doubt log; the checker matrix; the budgets and advisories; the agent incident ledger; the known limitations; and how to reproduce it.

Twelve rules govern the walk, and `statespace check report/` confirms each one.

| Rule | The report must |
|---|---|
| T1 | Put each claim once in the stamp sections, under its proposition-of-model stamp. REFUTED [R] results live with the specimens. |
| T2 | Print each line's stamp word, code and qualifier (the bound first for MODEL-CHECKED [M] and TESTED [T]), its label for where it was checked, its statement hash, its receipt ids and its three-segment strip. Requirement-fit is never PROVED [P]. |
| T3 | Turn each rests-on edge into an ASSUMED entry with its why, and each NOT COVERED [N] line into a NOT COVERED entry. |
| T4 | List the parts of a composite claim and give it no single stamp, least of all its strongest part's. |
| T5 | Move a claim whose statement hash no longer matches, or whose receipt fails re-verification, to UNKNOWN [?] with the reason. Nothing is dropped. |
| T6 | Keep a cracked claim's stamp and show the crack and the remedy. List every erosion in the log with both endpoints, its forecast result and its remedy. |
| T7 | Write "PROVED modulo k assumed lemmas", in words, when a Lean claim has k parked goals. Each lemma sits under ASSUMED with its parked date. |
| T8 | Print no bare "verified", "correct" or "safe", and no sum, average or percentage of stamps or of doubt counts. |
| T9 | Put design-level MODEL-CHECKED [M] claims and implementation-level TESTED [T] claims side by side, cross-linked, never merged. |
| T10 | Give the checker matrix one row per claim and one column per checker. Each cell is a verdict line with its receipt id, or blank. Nothing is inferred. |
| T11 | Print every bound and every tool version, with the advisories known on the report's date and each receipt's age limit, "stale since" once it has passed. |
| T12 | List every triaged proposal in the agent incident ledger: date, seed, operator family, verdict, class named and decision. |

## Doubts as m of n {minutes=13 kcs=KC-10.defeater-log}

A doubt is a reason why a claim might not hold. A claim with no doubts listed is not a claim without doubts. It is a claim nobody has looked at. The report keeps a doubt log for every claim, and the log has a shape.

There are three kinds of doubt, and they match the Audit Desk's gap classes. A rebutting doubt says the claim is false, and it comes as a counterexample. An undermining doubt says the evidence cannot be trusted: the solver, the encoding, the tool. An undercutting doubt says the reasoning from evidence to claim fails. Each doubt is active, retired, or an accepted residual. The log reads m|n: n doubts identified, m of them retired, and the other n − m named, active ones first.

Nine rules decide what happens to a doubt.

| Rule | What it says |
|---|---|
| D1 | Only a linked specimen, receipt or written argument retires a doubt. An AI may propose a doubt, labeled as its proposal, and never retires one. |
| D2 | A doubt that is not retired is active, unless you accept it as a residual, with a written rationale. |
| D3 | An argument is open while any doubt is active, or while any assumption it rests on reaches an unsupported claim or a crack with no remedy. Otherwise it is closed. |
| D4 | The log reads m|n, with the n − m others named. It is never summed, averaged, ranked or gated on. |
| D5 | An agreeing second checker retires a one-checker doubt and opens a doubt that both could fail together. An agreeing independent oracle retires a doubt about the specification only within its generator's distribution, and opens one for what lies outside it. |
| D6 | An erosion opens a doubt of the seam's kind on every claim that rests on the seam. The remedy retires it or restates it as a new assumption. |
| D7 | An advisory newer than a receipt opens a re-check doubt on every claim that cites that tool version. A receipt on a fixed version retires it. |
| D8 | A dismissed doubt keeps its record and rationale, and counts as retired only under D1. |
| D9 | The published report, CL-10.2, carries one standing doubt, "doubts not yet identified", accepted with a rationale that records the checklist you ran. It is never retired. |

To hunt doubts, go through every inference rule for undercutting doubts, every item of evidence for undermining ones, and every claim for counterexamples (Goodenough, Weinstock and Klein, 2015, section 3.1). The seven-category checklist from Gohar and colleagues' study of doubts in real safety cases is a good second pass.

## Publish what anyone can re-run {minutes=13 kcs=KC-10.publish-limitations}

A line in your report is reproducible only if a stranger can run it again and compare. So a receipt carries the tool and its version, the binary's hash, the argv, the hash of each input, the bound or seed, and the statement hash. Leave one out and the line says what you saw, not what anyone else can see.

The statement hash is what catches edits. It covers the statement's text with trailing whitespace removed, plus one newline, which matches the challenge hash of Comparator Live. A claim whose hash no longer matches, or whose receipt fails re-verification, moves to UNKNOWN [?] with the reason, and stays visible.

Receipts age. Each one carries a time to live, and past it the report prints "stale since". An advisory newer than a receipt opens a re-check doubt on every claim that cites the tool version.

The course pins the editions it recorded runs on: Lean {{run:RUN-lean-toolchain#version}} and PostgreSQL {{run:RUN-pg-claims#version}}. The PostgreSQL catalog is re-recorded after the next major release. Your report prints the editions you ran.

Write limitations in the open, as NOT COVERED lines for what lies outside and ASSUMED lines for what you depend on. CompCert's manual draws the same line between what its proof covers and what it does not (chapter 1, sections 1.2 and 1.3). Here is a finished line, from executed runs.

> CL-5.1 · MODEL-CHECKED [M] · constants Init = 100, Amount = 80, Workers = {w1, w2} · complete · {{run:RUN-tlc-atomic#values.w2-deadlock-off.distinct_states}} distinct states · TLC build {{run:RUN-tlc-jars#values.build_1_8_0}}, jar pinned by SHA-256, pre-release · argv recorded · statement hash printed {{run:RUN-tlc-jars}}. Also run at three workers with Amount = 40: {{run:RUN-tlc-atomic#values.w3-a40-deadlock-off.distinct_states}} distinct states {{run:RUN-tlc-atomic}}. Other sizes: NOT COVERED, unless the parametric step of CL-5.4 is proved.

Before you publish, `statespace check bundle/` also runs a privacy check: no private paths, secrets or identifiers that you did not choose to publish, and nothing from your private overlay. The bundle names the artifact and never you. Where to publish is your choice, and the check runs on the local bundle.

An agent commit has weakened the statement of `transfer_conserves`, under the same name, and `statespace check` has run comparator again.

:::instrument ins=INS-receipt cell=3 recorded=RUN-lean-comparator
```lean

    total (transfer s n) = total s

-- the agent's commit: the same name, one more hypothesis
```

@direction
The commit builds, because the weaker statement is true and its proof is complete. `statespace check` then compares the solution's statement with the pinned one, theorem by theorem, and the report is exported again.

@call key=C surprise=no run=RUN-lean-comparator
Where does CL-2.1 appear in the re-exported report?

- A. Under PROVED: the proof still builds.
  - why: The build proves the weakened statement, not the pinned one.
  - mis: dp:L3
- B. Under REFUTED: the statement changed.
  - why: REFUTED [R] needs a witness that violates the claim. Nothing was refuted.
  - mis: dp:X2
- C. Under UNKNOWN, with the reason "statement mismatch", until the pinned statement is proved again.
  - why: A statement mismatch voids the claim to UNKNOWN [?], and rule T5 keeps it visible.
- D. Nowhere: voided claims are dropped.
  - why: Dropping it would hide exactly the edit that comparator caught.
  - mis: dp:X9

@after
Comparator rejects the solution, because the statement does not match the pinned one {{run:RUN-lean-comparator}}. The build of the weakened theorem is still green: the kernel accepted a different statement. In the report, CL-2.1 moves to UNKNOWN [?] with the reason "statement mismatch", and it stays visible until the pinned statement is proved again (rule T5).
