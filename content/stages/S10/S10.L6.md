## Before you read the command {minutes=7 kcs=KC-10.agent-proposal-triage}

An agent's pull request adds one command and says: "Preserves conservation. 200 tests green." The claim sits in quotes, unreviewed and unstamped. What would it take for you to accept it?

You will answer that by running your own pipeline. This page walks the whole path once: read the claim, run your checker on it and on your invariants, decide, and then run the corrected command on PostgreSQL and record the result. Two questions first. The next segment answers both.

:::item role=prequestion family=GEN-call-it template=t4 kcs=KC-10.agent-proposal-triage
@choice key=C
Before you read the command, what do you need first?

- A. Whether the 200 tests cover the edge cases.
  - why: Tests sample behaviors. At best they give TESTED [T] · 200, and they do not say what was claimed.
  - mis: dp:X1
- B. Which tool the agent ran.
  - why: A tool's name is not evidence, and an agent's assertion is never stamped, whatever it cites.
  - mis: dp:X2
- C. Which of your invariants the command must preserve, and which checker decides each.
  - why: Triage starts from your own invariant set and from the kind of property each claim states, because a true claim can still be too weak.
- D. Whether it compiles.
  - why: Compiling establishes parsing and typing (S10.L1), not that an invariant is preserved.
  - mis: dp:S4
:::

## Read the claim, then your invariants {minutes=13 kcs=KC-10.agent-proposal-triage}

A proposal arrives as a command and a claim. Treat the claim as text under test: quote it, and say what kind of property it states before you run anything. The kind picks the checker.

| The claim says | Its kind | Who decides it |
|---|---|---|
| "No account goes negative" | A property of every reachable state | An obligation in Z3, then the induction check |
| "A self-transfer leaves the balance unchanged" | A property of one step, a state and its successor | An obligation over both states, in Z3 |
| "No tenant sees another's data" | A property of two runs together | Self-composition through your generator (LAB-7.5) |
| "Every hold is eventually resolved" | Something that eventually happens | Not Z3: a model check with its fairness stated |

The evidence the agent offers is a second thing to read, apart from the claim. "200 tests green" gives at best TESTED [T] · 200 with its generator statistics, and it does not say what was claimed. A tool's name gives nothing. An agent's assertion is never stamped, whatever it cites.

Then read your own invariants, before you read the command. The agent's claim is one property. Your Core holds a set of them, here conservation and a non-negative sender, and a true claim can still be too weak to protect that set. So the pipeline checks the agent's claim and your invariants side by side.

Your typechecker reads the command first, and a typed rejection is a valid outcome of triage. The command in the cell has no guard, so rule R-cover would turn it away before any solver ran. Read the cell as the question your checker asks of any version that gets past typing: what does this claim establish, and what does it leave out?

:::instrument ins=INS-z3 cell=1 recorded=RUN-z3-conservation-noguard
The proposal is `command Transfer(from, to, amount) do move amount from from to to`, with no guard. The claim is "conserves money".

```python
from z3 import *

a, b, amount = Ints('a b amount')        # two balances and the amount, as plain Int

# The agent's claim: the command conserves money.
print('conservation:')
prove((a - amount) + (b + amount) == a + b)

# Your other invariant: the sender never goes negative. The command has no guard.
o = Optimize()
o.add(a >= 0, b >= 0, amount > 0, a - amount < 0)
o.minimize(amount)
print('sender stays non-negative:', o.check())
print('smallest amount that breaks it:', o.model()[amount])
```

@direction
Query asserts: for any two `Int` balances and any amount, the negation of the agent's claim. `sat` means some input violates it. `unsat`, which `prove` prints as `proved`, means no input does, in this encoding. A second query asks the same of your own invariant, that the sender stays non-negative, and minimizes the amount.

@call key=B surprise=yes run=RUN-z3-conservation-noguard
What does Z3 return on the agent's claim, conservation over `Int`?

- A. `sat`
  - why: Over `Int`, (a − amount) + (b + amount) = a + b for every amount. The guard protects non-negativity, not conservation.
  - mis: dp:L4
- B. `unsat`, printed as `proved`
  - why: No input violates it in this encoding, if the solver is sound {{run:RUN-z3-conservation-noguard}}. That is true of the agent's claim, and it is not your requirement.
- C. `unknown`
  - why: Linear integer arithmetic is decidable here. `unknown` comes from nonlinear terms or a budget stop.
  - mis: dp:Z2

@after
Z3 proves the claim without a guard at all, because conservation over `Int` does not depend on one {{run:RUN-z3-conservation-noguard}}. Your second invariant fails. From a sender at 0, an amount of 1 breaks it, and no smaller positive amount exists {{run:RUN-z3-optimize-min}}. That is specimen SP-2.2. The claim is true and too weak. Reject the proposal for the invariant it ignores, and keep the claim's own line honest: its proposition holds in its model, and its requirement-fit does not. In the Audit Desk this is gap class G1, a statement too weak.
:::

- A. The evidence "200 tests green" earns TESTED [T] · 200 at most, never PROVED [P].
  - why: Tests sample behaviors. At most the claim carries the number of tests and the generator's statistics, and never a proof.
- B. The `unsat` shows that the command preserves your whole invariant set.
  - why: It answers the agent's one claim. Sender non-negativity is a different query, and without a guard the command breaks it {{run:RUN-z3-optimize-min}}.
  - mis: dp:L4
- C. The typechecker accepting the command shows that it preserves your invariant set.
  - why: Typing rules out ill-formed commands. It does not decide whether a well-formed command preserves an invariant.
  - mis: dp:S4

  - why: A witness earns REFUTED [R] only after it replays in the interpreter.
  - mis: dp:O5
:::

## Witness, then decide {minutes=13 kcs=KC-10.agent-proposal-triage}

A checker result is not yet a decision. Eliminative argumentation (Goodenough, Weinstock and Klein, 2015) names three ways to make counterevidence irrelevant, and they are the three decisions open to you:

- Change the system: fix the command, so that the witness no longer happens.
- Change the specification: narrow the claim honestly to a stated fragment. This is the roadmap's restricted obligation.
- Attack the counterevidence: a witness that does not replay is invalid, so suspect the encoding.

Two answers are not refutations at all. `unknown` fails closed, so you enforce at commit and do not guess (S4.L7). And a `sat` inductive step is not yet a bug, because its pre-state may be unreachable (S4.L6).

The agent now proposes a hold-capture command, with the claim that a capture never overdraws. Your invariant is `bal >= 0`, and its inductive step is satisfiable. A search from `Init` runs to a bound N that you print. Make your call before you run the cell, then try the twin: change the guard of `Hold` to `n <= bal` and run it again.

:::instrument ins=INS-z3 cell=2 recorded=RUN-z3-cti-pair
```python editable
from z3 import *

N = 6                                            # the search bound, which you print

def step(s, t, n):                               # the agent's commands: one step from s to t
    (bal, held), (bal2, held2) = s, t
    return And(n > 0, Or(
        And(n <= bal - held, held2 == held + n, bal2 == bal),    # Hold(n)
        And(n <= held, held2 == held - n, bal2 == bal - n),      # Capture(n)
        And(n <= held, held2 == held - n, bal2 == bal)))         # Release(n)

bal, held, bal2, held2, n = Ints('bal held bal2 held2 n')

# 1. The inductive step for bal >= 0: from any state that meets it, can one step break it?
q = Solver()
q.add(bal >= 0, step((bal, held), (bal2, held2), n), bal2 < 0)
print('step of bal >= 0:', q.check())
m = q.model()
print('  the pre-state has held above bal:', m[held].as_long() > m[bal].as_long())

# 2. A search from Init: a run of k steps that ends with bal < 0, for k up to N.
found = None
for k in range(N + 1):
    s = [(Int(f'bal{i}'), Int(f'held{i}')) for i in range(k + 1)]
    search = Solver()
    search.add(s[0][0] >= 0, s[0][1] == 0)                       # Init
    for i in range(k):
        search.add(step(s[i], s[i + 1], Int(f'n{i}')))
    search.add(s[k][0] < 0)                                      # the invariant fails at step k
    if search.check() == sat:
        found = k
        break
print('violation reachable within', N, 'steps:', 'no' if found is None else f'yes, at step {found}')

# 3. Strengthen the invariant to 0 <= held <= bal, and check that it closes.
inv = lambda b, h: And(0 <= h, h <= b)
q2 = Solver()
q2.add(inv(bal, held), step((bal, held), (bal2, held2), n), Not(inv(bal2, held2)))
print('step of the strengthened invariant:', q2.check())
i0 = Solver()
i0.add(bal >= 0, held == 0, Not(inv(bal, held)))
print('Init implies the strengthened invariant, as a violation query:', i0.check())
sf = Solver()
sf.add(inv(bal, held), Not(bal >= 0))
print('the strengthened invariant implies bal >= 0, as a violation query:', sf.check())
```

@direction
Query asserts: first, a state with `bal ≥ 0`, one step of the model and `bal < 0` afterwards. Then, for each k up to the bound, a run of k steps from `Init` that ends with `bal < 0`. Last, the same three checks for a stronger invariant: its step, `Init`, and that it implies `bal ≥ 0`. `sat` on the step query means a counterexample to induction exists. `sat` on a search query means a real run breaks the invariant.

@call key=C surprise=yes run=RUN-z3-cti-pair
The search finds no reachable violation within N. What do you do with the proposal?

- A. Reject the command: `sat` means a violation.
  - why: A `sat` inductive step is a counterexample to induction. It is a bug only if its pre-state is reachable.
  - mis: dp:O3
- B. Accept it and stamp PROVED [P]: nothing is reachable.
  - why: "None within N" is MODEL-CHECKED [M] · bound N, partial, never PROVED [P].
  - mis: dp:T5
- C. Keep the command, strengthen the invariant and check again.
  - why: The pre-state is unreachable within N, so this is a counterexample to induction. Once the strengthened invariant's step has no model, CL-4.4's PROVED [P] holds for the fragment {{run:RUN-z3-cti-pair}}.
- D. Mark it UNKNOWN [?] and enforce at commit.
  - why: `sat` is a definite answer. Enforcing is the fallback when proving fails, not the cure for a weak invariant.
  - mis: dp:O2

@after
The step query has a model, and in its pre-state the held funds exceed the balance. The search finds no run from `Init` that reaches a violation within the bound, so nothing says the command is wrong. Your invariant forgot a fact: held funds never exceed the balance. Strengthened to `0 ≤ held ≤ bal`, the step has no model, `Init` implies it and it implies `bal ≥ 0`, so the fragment earns CL-4.4's PROVED [P]. "None within N" on its own stays a bound. Run the twin and its search finds a run of three steps, so the same shape of counterexample then calls for a fix to the command {{run:RUN-z3-cti-pair}}.
:::

:::item role=segment family=GEN-spot-the-gap template=t3 kcs=KC-10.agent-proposal-triage
@choice key=D
An agent's pull request changes `Capture` to refuse when `n > bal - held`, and says: "Fixes the overdraft the checker found." The overdraft was the inductive step's counterexample for `bal >= 0`, and a search from `Init` finds no reachable violation within 6 steps. What is the gap in the pull request's claim?

- A. G1: the claim is too weak.
  - why: The claim is specific. The mistake is in what the overdraft was.
  - mis: dp:O2
- B. G4: the agent's encoding of `Capture` slipped.
  - why: The encoding matches the command. The state the solver found is a real state that no run reaches.
  - mis: dp:O5
- C. None: the change is sound, since a stricter guard cannot hurt.
  - why: A stricter guard can refuse commands your system needs, and it leaves the real weakness, an invariant that is not inductive, in place.
  - mis: dp:X2
- D. G13: the overdraft was a counterexample to induction, not a bug, so the change fixed nothing.
  - why: A counterexample to induction is a bug only if its pre-state is reachable, and the search found no run that reaches it. The remedy was to strengthen the invariant.
:::

## From the checker to PostgreSQL {minutes=13 kcs=KC-10.agent-proposal-triage}

A proved command is one claim of three. The command's claim is the obligation, proved for a stated fragment. The compiler's claim is that the SQL it emits means what the command means. The runtime's claim is that PostgreSQL behaves as documented under your retries. Each has its own stamp, and they stay apart (Stage 9).

That is why CL-10.1 is a composite. It lists REFUTED [R] or PROVED [P] for the command, TESTED [T] with n and generator statistics for the compiled runs, and TESTED [T] with its seeds for the simulation. It takes no single stamp, least of all the stamp of its strongest part.

The corrected self-transfer from the worked example shows the gap. Compile it two ways and run both on PostgreSQL, with account 1 at 100 and a self-transfer of 30. The cell is a recorded run, shown with its run ID. Make your call first.

:::instrument ins=INS-bench-pg cell=3 recorded=RUN-pg-claims
```sql
CREATE TABLE acct (id int PRIMARY KEY, bal bigint NOT NULL CHECK (bal >= 0));
INSERT INTO acct VALUES (1, 100);
-- compilation 1: one statement, from = to = 1, amount 30
UPDATE acct
   SET bal = CASE WHEN id = 1 THEN bal - 30
                  WHEN id = 1 THEN bal + 30 END
 WHERE id IN (1, 1);
SELECT id, bal FROM acct;
-- reset, then compilation 2: two statements
UPDATE acct SET bal = 100 WHERE id = 1;
UPDATE acct SET bal = bal - 30 WHERE id = 1 AND bal >= 30;
UPDATE acct SET bal = bal + 30 WHERE id = 1;
SELECT id, bal FROM acct;
```

@direction
Both compilations implement the same command. Compare the two final balances.

@call key=B surprise=no run=RUN-pg-claims
Compiled as one `UPDATE … SET bal = CASE … END` over both rows, the self-transfer commits. What is the final balance?

- A. 100
  - why: That is the two-UPDATE compilation. On one row, the first matching branch, `id = from`, applies only the debit.
  - mis: dp:P3
- B. 70
  - why: SP-6.7: the command's claim holds, and this compilation does not preserve its meaning {{run:RUN-pg-claims}}.
- C. 130
  - why: The credit branch is never reached once the debit branch matches.
  - mis: dp:X2
- D. An error: a value out of range
  - why: A value out of range is a {{run:RUN-pg-claims#values.e6_overflow_sqlstate}} failure {{run:RUN-pg-claims}}, and nothing here comes near the `bigint` limit.
  - mis: dp:Q6

@after
The one-statement compilation ended at {{run:RUN-pg-claims#values.e7_case_self_transfer_final}}, and the two-UPDATE compilation ended at {{run:RUN-pg-claims#values.e7_two_updates_final}}. The command is the same in both. The single matching row takes only the first true branch of the `CASE`, the debit, so 30 disappears. The command's claim held, and the compiler's claim failed. This is specimen SP-6.7.
:::

## Exit {minutes=4 kcs=KC-10.agent-proposal-triage}

:::item role=exit family=GEN-spot-the-gap template=t4 kcs=KC-10.agent-proposal-triage
@choice key=D
An agent's pull request rewrites the guard of `Transfer` from `balance(from) >= amount` to `amount <= balance(from)` and says: "Same behavior." Your checker proves the same obligations on both versions, each with a satisfiable premise witness, and no pinned statement changed. What is the gap?

- A. G1: the new guard is too weak.
  - why: The guard is the same inequality written the other way round.
  - mis: dp:X3
- B. G8a: the agent changed a pinned invariant in the same pull request.
  - why: The invariants and their statement hashes are untouched. Only the guard's spelling changed.
  - mis: dp:X2
- C. G11: the evidence is tests presented as proof.
  - why: The evidence is your own checker's, run on both versions.
  - mis: dp:X1
- D. None: the proposal is sound.
  - why: The guards are equivalent, every obligation is proved again with a premise witness, and no statement changed. Accepting a sound proposal is part of the job, and rejecting it by reflex is the other error.
:::

:::item role=exit family=GEN-which-stamp template=t3 kcs=KC-10.agent-proposal-triage
@choice key=C
The agent's claim for `Transfer` is PROVED [P], and your requirement that the sender stays non-negative fails for the command. Which segment of the claim's strip shows it?

- A. Proposition-of-model.
  - why: The proposition, conservation, holds in its model, so that segment reads PROVED [P] and stays so.
  - mis: dp:X8
- B. Correspondence.
  - why: Correspondence asks whether the code follows the model. Here the requirement is what fails, not the link to the code.
  - mis: dp:P3
- C. Requirement-fit.
  - why: A true claim can still be too weak for what you need, and requirement-fit is where the strip shows it. It is never PROVED [P].
- D. None: a PROVED [P] claim meets every requirement.
  - why: A proof is about its statement. It cannot show that the statement is the one you needed.
  - mis: dp:L3
