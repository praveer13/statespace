## Recall before you wire {minutes=3 kcs=KC-8.trace-validation,KC-8.deterministic-simulation,KC-6.differential-testing}

Your simulator replays a schedule of faults from a seed. Trace validation then asks TLC whether the trace your simulator produced is a behavior of your spec. Both are tests of your implementation, and a report that merges them with the spec's own model check loses what each one covers.

:::item role=prequestion family=GEN-call-it template=t1 kcs=KC-8.trace-validation,KC-8.deterministic-simulation,KC-6.differential-testing
@choice key=B
Your simulator's traces pass TLC trace validation on n seeds. What does the report print?

- A. PROVED [P]: no seed failed.
  - why: Sampled checks are never PROVED [P].
  - mis: dp:X1
- B. TESTED [T] · n seeds, composed with the spec's MODEL-CHECKED [M] result and never merged into it.
  - why: Trace validation over n seeds is a test of the implementation, set beside the spec's model check.
- C. MODEL-CHECKED [M] · n, because the seeds bound the exploration.
  - why: MODEL-CHECKED [M] belongs to the spec's model check. The implementation's traces are tested.
  - mis: dp:T5
:::

## Re-run your gate {minutes=6 kcs=KC-8.trace-validation,KC-8.deterministic-simulation,KC-6.differential-testing}

The gate from Stage 8 asks for two things (LAB-8.c7). Every seed is recorded, and every failure is mounted as a specimen. A seed makes a run replayable: the same seed gives the same schedule of faults and the same transitions. "Deterministic" does not mean "uses no randomness". It means the randomness comes from a seed you record, so a failure can be run again.

The wired suites print n, the seeds and the generator statistics. In the Core, the differential suite checks outcome classes against the interpreter, and the simulation suite checks traces against your spec. A failure in either is a witness. It becomes a specimen, labeled from the run that found it.

The cell is one trace from a recorded run, read by TLC's trace validation, one line at a time. Each line is an event of the outbox machine. TLC asks whether the event is a step your spec allows from the state so far, and stops at the first line that is not.

:::instrument ins=INS-bench-tlc cell=1 recorded=RUN-tlc-tracemodule call=false
```text
{"event":"Commit","cmd":"c1"}
{"event":"Commit","cmd":"c2"}
{"event":"Dispatch","cmd":"c1","key":"key-c1","att":1}
{"event":"TimerArmed","cmd":"c1"}
{"event":"Dispatch","cmd":"c2","key":"key-c2","att":1}
{"event":"Dispatch","cmd":"c1","key":"key-c1","att":2}
{"event":"Persist","cmd":"c2"}
{"event":"Ack","cmd":"c1"}
{"event":"Persist","cmd":"c1"}
{"event":"Ack","cmd":"c2"}
{"event":"End"}
```

@direction
Trace validation reads the lines in order. The trace ends with an End record, which tells TLC the log is complete and not cut short.

@after
Lines 1 to 7 conform. TLC rejects this trace at line {{run:RUN-tlc-tracemodule#values.rejected.first_non_conforming_line}} of {{run:RUN-tlc-tracemodule#values.rejected.lines}}: the acknowledgement of c1 arrives before its durable write {{run:RUN-tlc-tracemodule}}. A rejected trace earns no TESTED [T]. The trace and its first non-conforming line are the witness, and the seed that produced it becomes a specimen. A trace that stops early, with no End record, is only partial and earns nothing either. The n in the claim counts accepted traces only.
:::

Keep the two tests apart in your report. The spec's model check says what the design allows, over its constants. Trace validation says that your implementation's traces were behaviors of that design, on n seeds, and finite traces cannot test that something eventually happens. A claim about progress keeps its correspondence as an assumption.

If you ran LAB-8 on the course's reference module, swap in your own simulator now. Those modules earn nothing, and LAB-10.3 fails while any wired component is one.
