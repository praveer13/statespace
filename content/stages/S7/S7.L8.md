## Revocation over traces, then replicas {minutes=16 kcs=KC-7.revocation-past}

Revocation can promise two different things, and both are temporal statements, written as in S5. **S-new** says that after revocation no new read succeeds: `□[ReadOK(u, d) ⇒ canread(u, d)]_vars`, a safety property. **S-past** says that revocation removes what was already read: `□[Revoke(u, d) ⇒ d ∉ delivered′[u]]_vars`.

:::instrument ins=INS-interp cell=1 recorded=RUN-interp-revocation
The interpreter replays one trace on a single-copy model, where `Open` checks the current ACL, and evaluates both statements over it. `delivered[u]` is the log of every page returned to u.

```text
trace
  Share(contract -> dana)
  Open(dana, contract)
  Revoke(dana, contract)
  Open(dana, contract)

S-new   □[ReadOK(u, d) ⇒ canread(u, d)]_vars
S-past  □[Revoke(u, d) ⇒ d ∉ delivered′[u]]_vars
```

@direction
Trace asserts: a share, a read, a revocation and a second read. Each statement is judged over the behavior this trace produces.

@call key=A run=RUN-interp-revocation surprise=moderate
Is S-past refuted on this trace?

- A. Yes
  - why: After `Revoke`, dana's delivered log still holds the page.
- B. No
  - why: Revocation stops future reads only, and the statement asks for more.
  - mis: dp:A6

@after
The second `Open` is refused, so S-new holds on this trace, and dana's delivered log still holds the page after `Revoke`, so S-past is refuted {{run:RUN-interp-revocation}}. This is SP-7.3. Knowledge only grows, `□(d ∈ delivered[u] ⇒ □(d ∈ delivered[u]))`, so revocation can govern the future and nothing else. For the past, the release record's "when" says what holds: delivered stays delivered.
:::

::specimen id=SP-7.3

Zanzibar's paper gives the new enemy: "Bob should not be able to see the new contents, but may do so if the ACL check is evaluated with a stale ACL from before Bob's removal" (§2.2). Here `Open` checks the ACL on a lagging replica and reads content from the primary.

:::instrument ins=INS-interp cell=2 recorded=RUN-interp-new-enemy
`Sync` copies the primary's ACL to the replica. gia revokes dana and edits contract on the primary, and dana opens contract.

```text
trace
  Share(contract -> dana)
  Sync
  Revoke(dana, contract)
  Edit(contract, body = "v2")
  Open(dana, contract)

Open(u, d)  checks the ACL on the replica, reads the content from the primary
```

@direction
Trace asserts: the replica last synced before the revocation, then one edit on the primary and one read by dana.

@call key=B run=RUN-interp-new-enemy surprise=yes
What can dana see?

- A. Nothing: she was revoked.
  - why: The check consulted a copy that had not heard of the revocation.
  - mis: dp:A14
- B. The new body.
  - why: A stale ACL was applied to new content.
- C. The old body only.
  - why: The ACL and the content come from different copies here.
  - mis: dp:A14

@after
S-new fails on the replicated model {{run:RUN-interp-new-enemy}}. This is SP-7.6, and it is an erosion: CL-6.3's assumption that nothing reads a replica cracks, while CL-6.2 does not rest on that clause and stands. Three remedies: check the ACL and read the content at one snapshot; keep a consistency token with each content version and require an ACL snapshot at least as fresh; or route reads after an ACL change to the primary. The claim records the choice as [E] where it is enforced and as [A] otherwise.
:::

::specimen id=SP-7.6

:::item role=segment family=GEN-trace-allowed template=t1 kcs=KC-7.revocation-past
@choice key=B
On the replicated model, where `Open` checks the replica and `Sync` copies the primary's ACL to it, which trace violates S-new?

- A. `Share(dana) · Open(dana) · Revoke(dana)`
  - why: No read follows the revocation. This trace breaks S-past, not S-new.
  - mis: dp:A6
- B. `Share(dana) · Sync · Revoke(dana) · Open(dana)`
  - why: The replica still lists dana, so the read succeeds after the revocation.
- C. None: the check reads an ACL, so no read after a revocation succeeds.
  - why: The ACL it reads may be a stale copy.
  - mis: dp:A14
:::

## Exit {minutes=4 kcs=KC-7.revocation-past}

:::item role=exit family=GEN-call-it template=t4 kcs=KC-7.revocation-past
@choice key=C
Which statement holds for a system whose ACL check reads a replica?

- A. Once `Revoke` returns, no later read by that user succeeds.
  - why: A read can succeed until the replica hears of the revocation.
  - mis: dp:A14
- B. Whatever the user read before the revocation is erased.
  - why: Delivered information stays delivered.
  - mis: dp:A6
- C. A later read can succeed until the replica hears of the revocation, and what was already read stays known.
  - why: Both halves follow from the model: the check reads a copy, and knowledge only grows.
:::
