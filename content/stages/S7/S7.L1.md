## The nouns {minutes=3 kcs=KC-7.access-models}

Your system now has tenants, teams, users, roles, documents and sharing. The roadmap states the read rule in one sentence: a user may see a document when they are a team member or it is explicitly shared. Whatever no rule allows is denied.

:::item role=prequestion family=GEN-call-it template=t1 kcs=KC-7.access-models
@choice key=B
frank is in Acme, on Growth. q3-plan belongs to Payments, also in Acme, and nobody has shared it with him. Under the roadmap's rule, in a system that denies by default, can frank see q3-plan?

- A. Yes: nothing in the rule forbids it.
  - why: A default-deny system allows exactly what some rule derives.
  - mis: dp:A7
- B. No: neither way in applies to him.
  - why: He is not on Payments and holds no share.
- C. Yes: he and Payments share a tenant.
  - why: No rule mentions tenants, so sharing one grants nothing.
  - mis: dp:A2
:::

## Reading a rule on a world {minutes=12 kcs=KC-7.access-models}

A policy here is facts and rules. A fact records one relationship: a membership, an owner, a share. A rule says when a new fact follows from others. The engine derives every fact the rules allow and nothing else, which is default deny. A premise written `!revoked(d, u)` must be absent.

Rules differ in what they consult. A role-based rule asks what you are: admins may delete. An attribute-based rule compares properties: clearance at or above the label. A relationship-based rule follows a path through the data, from user to team to document. The roadmap's rule is relationship-based, and it has three ways in.[^1]

[^1]: In Zanzibar the recursion in such paths lives in the engine, in Cedar in the data (each entity carries its ancestors), and in Datalog in the rules. S7.L3 shows why that matters to a checker.

The toy below relates users straight to documents. Make your call before you run it.

:::instrument ins=INS-datalog cell=1 recorded=RUN-z3-fixedpoint
```datalog
.decl member(u: number, d: number)
.decl shared(u: number, d: number)
.decl revoked(u: number, d: number)
.decl canread(u: number, d: number)
.output canread

member(1, 3).
shared(1, 4).
revoked(1, 4).

canread(u, d) :- member(u, d).
canread(u, d) :- shared(u, d), !revoked(u, d).
```

@direction
Query asks: is `canread(1, 4)` derivable? `sat` means yes, so user 1 may read document 4.

@call key=B run=RUN-z3-fixedpoint surprise=yes
What does the engine answer for `canread(1, 4)`?

- A. `sat`
  - why: `shared(1, 4)` is one premise, and rule 2 also needs `revoked(1, 4)` absent.
  - mis: dp:A8
- B. `unsat`
  - why: Rule 1 needs `member(1, 4)`, and rule 2's negated premise fails.
- C. `unknown`
  - why: A program with negation over finitely many facts reaches its fixpoint in finitely many steps.
  - mis: dp:A9

@after
In Datalog, `sat` means derivable, which here means allowed. In S1, after you asserted a negation, `sat` meant a violation existed. Read the polarity each time.
:::

The roadmap's world, W0, adds teams, owners and tenants. Its rules are the three ways in.

:::instrument ins=INS-datalog cell=2 recorded=RUN-datalog-matrix
```datalog editable
.decl tenant_of_team(t: symbol, a: symbol)
.decl tenant_of_user(u: symbol, a: symbol)
.decl member(u: symbol, t: symbol, r: symbol)
.decl owns(t: symbol, d: symbol)
.decl share_user(d: symbol, u: symbol)
.decl share_team(d: symbol, t: symbol)
.decl revoked(d: symbol, u: symbol)
.decl canread(u: symbol, d: symbol)
.output canread

// the world: two tenants, three teams, four users, four documents
tenant_of_team("payments", "acme").  tenant_of_team("growth", "acme").  tenant_of_team("legal", "birch").
tenant_of_user("dana", "acme").  tenant_of_user("frank", "acme").
tenant_of_user("gia", "birch").  tenant_of_user("hal", "birch").
member("dana", "payments", "editor").  member("frank", "growth", "viewer").
member("gia", "legal", "admin").       member("hal", "legal", "viewer").
owns("payments", "q3-plan").  owns("growth", "roadmap").
owns("legal", "contract").    owns("legal", "layoffs-memo").
share_user("contract", "dana").
share_team("roadmap", "payments").

// rule 1: a member of the owning team
canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, _), owns(t, d).
// rule 2: shared with the user, and not revoked
canread(u, d) :- share_user(d, u), !revoked(d, u).
// rule 3: shared with one of the user's teams
canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, _), share_team(d, t).
```

@direction
Query asks: which (user, document) pairs are derivable? The cell prints `canread` as a matrix of users and documents.

@call key=A run=RUN-datalog-matrix surprise=yes
dana is in Acme. contract belongs to Birch. Can dana read contract?

- A. Yes
  - why: Rule 2 fires on `share_user("contract", "dana")`, and no rule mentions tenants.
- B. No
  - why: Tenants are not walls, and Birch chose to share contract.
  - mis: dp:A15

@after
Your system will need exactly this: a document that crosses a tenant on purpose. S7.L7 comes back to it.
:::

:::item role=segment family=GEN-call-it template=t4 kcs=KC-7.access-models
@choice key=C
Edit the program: Birch revokes dana's share of contract, and shares layoffs-memo with Growth. Which statement is true?

- A. dana can still read contract, because it is shared with her.
  - why: Rule 2 also needs the revocation absent, and it is now present.
  - mis: dp:A8
- B. frank can read q3-plan, because no rule forbids it.
  - why: Whatever no rule derives is denied.
  - mis: dp:A7
- C. frank can read layoffs-memo, a Birch document.
  - why: Rule 3 applies: layoffs-memo is shared with Growth, and frank is on Growth.
- D. No Acme user can read a Birch document, because tenants are walls.
  - why: The share to Growth crosses the tenant boundary, as the share to dana did.
  - mis: dp:A15
:::

## Exit {minutes=3 kcs=KC-7.access-models}

:::item role=exit family=GEN-call-it template=t4 kcs=KC-7.access-models
@choice key=C
Which of these rules is relationship-based?

- A. Admins may delete any document.
  - why: It consults a role and nothing else, so it is role-based.
  - mis: dp:A2
- B. A user may read a document whose label is at or below their clearance.
  - why: It compares properties of the user and the document, so it is attribute-based.
  - mis: dp:A2
- C. A user may read a document shared with any team they belong to.
  - why: It follows a path from user to team to document, as rule 3 does.
:::
