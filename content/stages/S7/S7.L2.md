## A complete justification {minutes=2 kcs=KC-7.policy-as-datalog}

In S7.L1 you read a policy. Now you write one, and you make it explain itself.

:::item role=prequestion family=GEN-find-the-witness template=t1 kcs=KC-7.policy-as-datalog
@choice key=B
The engine says dana may read contract. Which justification is complete?

- A. `share_user("contract", "dana")` is a fact.
  - why: Rule 2 also needs `revoked("contract", "dana")` absent.
  - mis: dp:A8
- B. Rule 2, applied to `share_user("contract", "dana")`, with `revoked("contract", "dana")` not derivable.
  - why: Every premise of the rule instance is accounted for.
- C. Birch lets Acme users read its documents.
  - why: There is no tenant-level grant in the rules.
  - mis: dp:A2
:::

## Rules and the matrix {minutes=9 kcs=KC-7.policy-as-datalog}

A worked example first. Rule 1 reads: `canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, _), owns(t, d).` User u may read document d if u is a member of some team t, in any role (the `_` ignores it), and t owns d. The variable `t` joins the two premises, so it must be one team in both.

Rules 2 and 3 have the same shape. Rule 2 adds a negated premise, `!revoked(d, u)`. Soufflé's rules page sets two conditions for negation: a negated literal binds no variable, so `d` and `u` must already appear in a positive premise, and the program must be stratifiable, with no relation depending on its own negation through a cycle. Run all three rules on W0.

:::instrument ins=INS-datalog cell=1 recorded=RUN-datalog-matrix
```datalog editable
.decl member(u: symbol, t: symbol, r: symbol)
.decl owns(t: symbol, d: symbol)
.decl share_user(d: symbol, u: symbol)
.decl share_team(d: symbol, t: symbol)
.decl revoked(d: symbol, u: symbol)
.decl canread(u: symbol, d: symbol)
.output canread

member("dana", "payments", "editor").  member("frank", "growth", "viewer").
member("gia", "legal", "admin").       member("hal", "legal", "viewer").
owns("payments", "q3-plan").  owns("growth", "roadmap").
owns("legal", "contract").    owns("legal", "layoffs-memo").
share_user("contract", "dana").
share_team("roadmap", "payments").

// rule 1: a member of the owning team
canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, _), owns(t, d).
// rule 2: shared with the user, and not revoked
canread(u, d) :- share_user(d, u), !revoked(d, u).
// rule 3: shared with one of the user's teams
canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, _), share_team(d, t).
```

@direction
Query asks: which (user, document) pairs are derivable under rules 1 to 3? The cell draws `canread` as the access matrix.

@call key=B run=RUN-datalog-matrix surprise=no
Add `revoked("contract", "dana")`. Which cells change?

- A. None: the share is still there.
  - why: The revocation removes rule 2's other premise.
  - mis: dp:A8
- B. Exactly the dana-contract cell.
  - why: dana is not in Legal, and contract has no team share.
- C. Every cell of contract.
  - why: A revocation names one user.
  - mis: dp:A2

@after
You changed one fact, and the engine recomputed every consequence. Rules and facts stay separate.
:::

:::item role=segment family=GEN-spec-it template=t2 kcs=KC-7.policy-as-datalog mode=pick
@choice key=A
Translate: "Editors of the owning team may read a document, and so may anyone it is shared with, unless that share is revoked." Which rules say it?

- A. `canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, "editor"), owns(t, d).` and `canread(u, d) :- share_user(d, u), !revoked(d, u).`
  - why: Two ways in, each with its own premises, and the revocation guards the share.
- B. `canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, "editor"), owns(t, d).` and `canread(u, d) :- share_user(d, u).`
  - why: The second rule ignores revocations, so a revoked share still allows the read.
  - mis: dp:A8
- C. `canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, "editor"), owns(t, d), share_user(d, u), !revoked(d, u).`
  - why: One rule joins the two ways in, so it demands both at once.
  - mis: dp:A2
:::

## Derivations {minutes=9 kcs=KC-7.policy-as-datalog}

An allowed cell has a derivation: a tree whose leaves are facts and whose nodes are rule instances. Soufflé prints trees of minimal height. A denied cell has no tree, because nothing derived it. To justify a denial you go through the rules and name, for each, the first premise that finds no match. Soufflé's provenance page says it cannot generate explanations for non-existence automatically.

:::instrument ins=INS-datalog cell=2 recorded=RUN-datalog-matrix
```datalog
.decl member(u: symbol, t: symbol, r: symbol)
.decl owns(t: symbol, d: symbol)
.decl share_user(d: symbol, u: symbol)
.decl share_team(d: symbol, t: symbol)
.decl revoked(d: symbol, u: symbol)
.decl canread(u: symbol, d: symbol)
.output canread

member("dana", "payments", "editor").  member("frank", "growth", "viewer").
member("gia", "legal", "admin").       member("hal", "legal", "viewer").
owns("payments", "q3-plan").  owns("growth", "roadmap").
owns("legal", "contract").    owns("legal", "layoffs-memo").
share_user("contract", "dana").
share_team("roadmap", "payments").

canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, _), owns(t, d).
canread(u, d) :- share_user(d, u), !revoked(d, u).
canread(u, d) :- member(u, t, _), share_team(d, t).
```

@direction
Query asks: explain `canread("dana", "contract")` with a derivation, and `canread("frank", "contract")` rule by rule.

@call key=B run=RUN-datalog-matrix surprise=moderate
Can a denial be justified by one tree, the way an allowance can?

- A. Yes
  - why: Absence is not a fact, so it has no proof tree.
  - mis: dp:A9
- B. No
  - why: A denial says no instance of any rule applies, so you go rule by rule.

@after
For dana and contract, Soufflé prints a one-leaf tree: `share_user("contract", "dana")` under the share rule, which it labels `R3` by its own numbering. The tree leaves out the negated premise, so a complete justification adds that `revoked("contract", "dana")` is not derivable. For frank and contract it prints `Tuple not found` {{run:RUN-datalog-matrix}}. For frank and contract, rule 1 matches frank's team, but Growth does not own contract. Rule 2 finds no share to frank. Rule 3 finds contract shared with no team. Three rules, three first failures.
:::

:::item role=segment family=GEN-find-the-witness template=t1 kcs=KC-7.policy-as-datalog
@choice key=A
Why can't frank read contract? Pick the complete account.

- A. No rule applies: frank is not on Legal (rule 1), no share names him (rule 2), and contract is shared with none of his teams (rule 3).
  - why: Each rule is accounted for by its first failing premise.
- B. frank is in Acme and contract belongs to Birch, so the tenants differ.
  - why: No rule mentions tenants, and a share could still cross that boundary.
  - mis: dp:A15
- C. Rule 2 fails because contract is revoked for frank.
  - why: Nothing is revoked for frank; rule 2 fails because no share names him.
  - mis: dp:A8
:::

## Exit {minutes=3 kcs=KC-7.policy-as-datalog}

:::item role=exit family=GEN-find-the-witness template=t1 kcs=KC-7.policy-as-datalog
@choice key=B
Which derivation justifies `canread("dana", "roadmap")` on W0?

- A. Rule 1, on `member("dana", "payments", "editor")` and `owns("growth", "roadmap")`.
  - why: The variable `t` must be one team in both premises, and dana's team does not own roadmap.
  - mis: dp:A2
- B. Rule 3, on `member("dana", "payments", "editor")` and `share_team("roadmap", "payments")`.
  - why: dana is on Payments and roadmap is shared with Payments, so every premise is a fact.
- C. Rule 2, on `share_user("roadmap", "dana")`, with `revoked("roadmap", "dana")` not derivable.
  - why: No fact shares roadmap with dana as a user, so the rule has no instance.
  - mis: dp:A8
:::
