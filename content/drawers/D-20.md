## A cache is an observer {minutes=2}

Anything that stores a response and serves it to someone else observes it. Widen the observer to include what shared caches keep, and noninterference has to hold for that wider observer (lesson S7.L6).

This card names no vendor and no deployment: the roadmap defers edge and CDN topology (D-37).

## What the standard says {minutes=2}

RFC 9111 says a shared cache must not store a response marked `private`, and it adds that the directive only controls where the response can be stored: it cannot ensure the privacy of the message content. A directive you set and read back is CONFIGURED [C]. The property it is meant to protect still needs its own stamp.

## Paired worlds {minutes=2}

Two worlds differ only in another tenant's secret. The observer sees the responses, and what a shared cache returns for them. A cache key that omits the tenant, or a difference between a hit and a miss, lets the observer tell the worlds apart.
