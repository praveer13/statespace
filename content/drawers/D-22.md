## What it is, and how to check it {minutes=3}

A syntax category plus macros lets `Transfer(a, b, 5)` appear in a Lean file. Macros rewrite syntax before elaboration, so the syntax tree and its types are unchanged. You do not need this: the course's parser is your Rust one (the last stage of LAB-3, and the first step of the capstone), and the toy language skips parsing (lesson S3.L1), so most learners never meet this drawer.

Check an embedding with a golden: a kernel-checked equation between the embedded form and the tree you meant, closed by `rfl` or `decide`, shows the macro expands as intended. The exact syntax depends on your definitions.

## Metaprograms run when you build {minutes=3}

Macros and other metaprograms run inside the Lean process during elaboration. The advisory for a Lean kernel soundness bug says that exploiting it takes running a metaprogram inside that process, for example by building a project or importing a dependency {{fact:incident.lean-kernel-14576}}. That is why `lake check` sandboxes the build and replays an exported proof through the kernel (lesson S2.L9). Your macros are code the build runs.

The roadmap lists Lean 4 metaprogramming for Stage 9 "selectively", and this drawer is the selection.
