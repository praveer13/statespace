## Replicas and order {minutes=3}

A replica that has not yet applied an access change can serve a read after the revocation committed. Google's Zanzibar paper calls this the "new enemy" problem. Three remedies appear in lesson S7.L8: read the permissions and the content at one snapshot; carry a consistency token with each content version and require a permissions snapshot at least as fresh (Zanzibar's zookies); or route reads after a permissions change to the primary. Revocation stops future reads. It cannot recall what was already delivered {{run:RUN-interp-revocation}}.

A history is linearizable when some order that respects real time explains every result. byzantine's checker searches for such an order, and the serial-order search in lesson S6.L8 does the same for committed transactions.

## The deterministic harness {minutes=3}

byzantine's labs drive a simulated network from seeded random streams, and the harness, not the wall clock, fires timers. The same seed gives the same transitions, so a failing seed replays. To read a fault schedule, take the seed, then each injected fault (drop, delay, duplicate, crash) in order, then the first step where an invariant fails.

Where your own simulator can break this: route every nondeterministic choice through the seed. Reading the system clock breaks replay, and so does iterating a Rust `HashMap`, whose default hasher is randomly seeded. Deterministic means seeded, not free of randomness.

A byzantine lab passed on unseen seeds counts as test-out evidence for the deterministic simulation idea.
