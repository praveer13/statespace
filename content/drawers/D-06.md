## Rule 3 and your first card {minutes=4}

The roadmap's third rule: "Explain every guarantee as: proposition, assumptions, model, checking method, implementation correspondence, and counterexample when an assumption is weakened." Your first claim card, as lesson S1.L3 mints it from your results, shows each field and why it exists:

| Field | CL-1.1 | Why the field exists |
|---|---|---|
| Proposition | for all a, b, amount in ℤ: a ≥ 0 ∧ b ≥ 0 ∧ amount > 0 ∧ amount ≤ a → a − amount ≥ 0 | exactly what was checked, no more |
| Assumptions | balances are unbounded integers, ASSUMED [A], a seam; the solver is sound, ASSUMED [A], a seam | what the result rests on; seams are planted on purpose, and some erode later |
| Model | one step over Z3's `Int`; the statement file `claims/cl-1-1.smt2`, hashed on the receipt | the encoding is the claim (lesson S1.L5) |
| Method | the violation query comes back `unsat` {{run:RUN-z3-starter}}; the premise query comes back `sat` {{run:RUN-z3-vacuous-unsat}}; PROVED [P] of the encoding, with checker, version and budget named, uncertified | which checker, which version, which bound, and the premise witness (D-02) |
| Correspondence | no code named yet: ASSUMED [A] that the code computes the subtraction without overflow, with the open defeater "encoding unverified" | which code the model stands for; usually the weakest link |
| If weakened | drop the guard and the solver finds a witness again {{run:RUN-z3-starter#values.model}} | shows that the assumptions carry weight; the card's own check confirms that the witness refutes the claim's proposition |

Below the six fields sits the requirement row: "no transfer leaves the paying account below zero", ASSUMED [A], with the recipient NOT COVERED [N].

## The strip, and what a claim is not {minutes=3}

A claim renders as three segments: proposition of the model (what the checker established about the model), correspondence (whether the model matches the code) and requirement fit (whether the proposition is what you wanted). Requirement fit is never PROVED [P]. Ask who wrote the statement: an agent may draft a claim, but its assertions render as quoted UNREVIEWED text and are never stamped.

A claim records what an artifact establishes, and agents may produce one, so claims never move "signed off". Unassisted, checker-graded work on unseen seeds does: your items, your probes and the Defence.

For Rust readers: an `unsafe fn`'s `# Safety` section lists what callers must guarantee, and a claim card is that section made complete. Where it breaks: nothing checks a `# Safety` comment, while `statespace check` validates every field of a card against its receipt.
